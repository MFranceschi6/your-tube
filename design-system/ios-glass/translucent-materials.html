YourTube · iOS · Translucent Materials Layer

A glass material vocabulary for YourTube iOS.

An original layered translucent-materials language for the iOS build of YourTube. It sits on top of the existing brand tokens (purple accent, type scale, spacing) and re-renders the chrome surfaces — tab bar, MiniPlayer, NowPlaying controls, sheets, menus, toasts — as stacked glass: backdrop blur + tinted fill + specular highlight + refracted edge + depth shadow. Each surface has dark and light tokens and is annotated with the trade-off that comes with going translucent.

Not a platform clone. This is YourTube's own translucent layer, not a recreation of any vendor's OS chrome. Apply it inside the YourTube app shell only — do not present these surfaces as system UI.
01 · Foundations
Material weights

Four weights — thin, regular, thick, ultra — over the same backdrop. Each weight pairs a fill opacity with a blur radius. The remaining four layers (saturation boost, specular highlight, refracted edge, depth shadow) are constants of the system.

The five layers (constant across all weights)

1 · Backdrop blur of the content underneath. The blur radius is what gives the material its softness.

2 · Fill — a semi-opaque color (dark for dark theme, white for light theme). The opacity is what gives the material its weight.

3 · Specular highlight — a 135° linear gradient at 6–10% white from the top-left. Gives the surface a sense of being glossy rather than flat.

4 · Refracted edge — asymmetric inner stroke. Bright on top (where light would land), dark on bottom (where it would fall away). This is the move that reads most as "glass" rather than "blur".

5 · Depth shadow — soft outer shadow so the material reads as floating, not flush.

When to use which

thin — chrome you want to read THROUGH (tab bar over scrolling content).

regular — primary interactive chrome (MiniPlayer, sheets, NowPlaying controls).

thick — focused content where readability beats transparency (context menus, alerts).

ultra — modal blockers, settings sheets at full height.

Trade-off · contrast
All four materials must pass WCAG AA against the worst-case backdrop. We test white-on-glass over a saturated artwork. If contrast falls below 4.5:1, promote the material one weight. Respect UIAccessibilityIsReduceTransparencyEnabled — when enabled, every glass surface flattens to --color-surface with no blur.
Trade-off · performance
Each glass surface is a paint barrier. On older iPhones, more than ~3 simultaneous large blurred surfaces (tab bar + sheet + nested menu) drops the framerate. Architect screens so only one "layer of intent" is glass at a time — when a sheet opens, freeze the tab bar's blur to a snapshot.
Trade-off · battery
Backdrop blur is GPU-bound and modestly battery-positive on modern hardware, but it compounds. The MiniPlayer is on-screen 80% of the time the app is open — keep it at regular, never thick.

Why floating, not flush

The tab bar is detached from the screen edges with 16pt insets and pill geometry. This makes the translucency legible — flush-to-edge tab bars read as opaque even when they're not.

Active state

Active tab swaps icon + label color to --color-accent. No glass-color tricks. The inactive tabs use 55% white in dark, 45% black in light.

Trade-off
Floating pill takes 16pt of horizontal space and 8pt extra vertical space vs. a flush tab bar. On smaller iPhones (SE class) this is non-trivial — fall back to a flush thick tab bar on devices < 5.4".

Two stacked glass surfaces

The MiniPlayer (regular) sits 8pt above the tab bar (thin). Two glass surfaces is the upper limit before the stack starts looking smudgy — never add a third on the same screen. Both must be on screen for a "stack discount": the tab bar samples the MiniPlayer's already-blurred output, so its blur radius can drop to 12px without losing the effect.

Progress as edge

The progress fill rides the bottom inner edge of the pill — same purple as the active tab. It re-uses the existing --color-scrubber-fill token; no new color.

Trade-off · always-on cost
The MiniPlayer is on screen 80%+ of the time the app is open. regular is the ceiling — never thick. If a track has no artwork, drop the glass entirely and use opaque --color-surface — there's nothing colorful underneath to make the translucency worth the cost.

Backdrop is content, chrome is glass

The blurred artwork is the room; the controls are translucent floating panels in that room. Importantly, the artwork card itself is opaque — only the chrome is glass. Glass-on-glass would be illegible.

Tinting from artwork

Unlike the rest of the app where chrome is neutral, here the glass deliberately picks up the artwork color. The blur is doing the tinting, not a separate tint layer — keep the chrome material neutral and let the content do the work.

Play/Pause as a glass dot

The big circular play button is itself a glass surface. Press state drops it to glass-flat shadow + 0.97 scale.

Trade-off · text contrast
A vivid artwork can blow out the white track title. Always render a 0.5 black gradient (40% → 0%) directly above the meta block before it lands on the artwork. This is non-negotiable for albums with very bright covers.

Stack: header on top of search

Two pills stacked, 8pt gap. The header is shorter (40pt) and only carries back/title/overflow; the search is taller (44pt) so the input has minimum tap target. Both glass.

Scroll-edge effect

When content scrolls under the headers, the blur picks up new colors. Don't add a scroll-edge gradient mask on top — the glass already does the separation work.

Trade-off · keyboard inset
When the search field is focused and the keyboard rises, the floating pill must rise with it (or it will look detached over the keyboard). Easier in UIKit than SwiftUI; budget half a day for this.

Why thick, not regular

A sheet steals focus. The user is committing to a sub-task — Add to playlist, Share, Settings. Translucency through to the dimmed feed below would be visual noise. thick says "this is the new context".

Grabber as the only "system" element

4pt × 36pt rounded bar at the top of the sheet, 40% white in dark, 25% black in light. This is the universal "drag me down" affordance — keep it.

Destructive actions

Use --color-error for both icon and label. Always at the bottom of the sheet, separated by a divider. Never on the same row as a constructive action.

Trade-off · scrim or no scrim
If the sheet is full-height (settings, queue), use ultra material and skip the scrim. If it's a partial sheet (action picker, share), use thick with a 40% scrim. Half-measures look uncertain.

Source row stays in focus

The row that triggered the menu scales 1.02 and stays sharp. Every other row dims to 40% opacity and blurs 2px. The menu floats next to the focused row, not centered.

Grouping with dividers

Constructive actions on top, share in the middle, destructive at the bottom. Dividers between groups (10% white in dark, 10% black in light) — not between every item.

Shortcut hints

Hardware-keyboard shortcuts (iPad) are right-aligned in tertiary text. Trailing affordance icons live in the same column.

Trade-off · width
240pt is the sweet spot for two-line items at 15pt body. Don't go below 220pt or labels truncate; don't go above 280pt or the menu reads as a sheet.
08 · Feedback
Toast / inline confirmation

Transient confirmation pill — appears for ~2.5s above the MiniPlayer. thick material so the message is readable; glass-pill geometry; subtle entry animation (slide-up 8pt + fade).

Above the MiniPlayer

Position fixed at bottom: tabbar + miniplayer + 16pt. Toast must never overlap the controls — it's confirmation, not a focus-stealer.

Auto-dismiss

2.5s default; 4s if there's a Retry action. Tap dismisses. Slide-up 8pt + fade-in over 200ms; reverse on dismiss.

One at a time

Toasts queue, never stack. New toast pushes the current one out (slide-up + fade) before appearing.

Trade-off · don't replace alerts
Toasts are confirmation, not consent. A destructive action (Delete playlist) still requires a real alert with a Cancel button — never a toast with an Undo. Undo-toast UX punishes the 90% of users who didn't make a mistake.

One layer of intent at a time

Total surface count rule: ≤ 3 glass layers per screen. If you need more, something is in the wrong place.

10 · Summary
Should YourTube ship this?

Pros

· The MiniPlayer + tab bar pair is the clearest win. Two persistent surfaces that float above scrolling content benefit most from translucency — they read as "always there but never opaque-blocking".

· NowPlaying gets a real visual upgrade. Blurred artwork as backdrop with translucent chrome over it is the natural place for this language.

· Only one new file — glass_tokens.css — and it composes additively on the existing tokens. Roll-back is one toggle.

Cons / risks

· Performance ceiling. Stacking more than 3 large blurred surfaces drops below 60fps on iPhone 12 and older. Architectural discipline required, not just visual.

· Contrast policing. Every glass surface needs an automated contrast test against worst-case backdrops. Without it you ship a beautiful design that fails AA.

· Reduce-transparency. Real users turn this on. Every glass surface must have a fully-opaque fallback. ~15% extra design work.

· Android divergence. Material You doesn't have an analog. Don't try to port it. The two platforms get different chrome — that's fine.

Recommendation

Ship it for iOS only, gated behind the existing translucent code paths in MiniPlayer (already backdrop-filter: blur(20px) in the original system). Start with 03 MiniPlayer + 02 tab bar + 04 NowPlaying. Defer 06 sheets and 07 menus to a second pass — they're lower-frequency surfaces.
